The Chemistry of Cold Brew Extraction: Low Acidity & Sweetness

Unlike hot drip extraction, cold-water immersion steeping over 14 to 18 hours reduces the extraction of bitter chlorogenic acid derivatives by up to 66%. Instead, temperature-resistant lipid compounds and natural sucrose slowly dissolve over time. This creates a gentle, smooth profile that is easy on the stomach and develops wine-like mellow undertones upon refrigerated aging.

1. Selecting Your Immersion Ratio: Concentrate vs RTD

☕ 1 : 5 (Ultra-Dense Concentrate)
100g coffee to 500ml water yields ~300ml of viscous espresso-strength liquid. Ideal for rich iced lattes, oat milk pairings, or drizzled over vanilla gelato for an artisanal affogato.

🧊 1 : 8 (Standard Golden Concentrate)
The gold-standard ratio favored by specialty coffee shops. Adding 800ml water to 100g coffee creates a versatile base. Diluting 1:1 or 1:2 with water, tonic, or sparkling soda makes a crisp, refreshing iced coffee.

🥤 1 : 12 (Ready-to-Drink RTD)
Pours directly from bottle to glass with zero dilution required. Elegant, tea-like mouthfeel with delicate lingering sweetness.

2. Three Golden Rules of Flawless Immersion

  • Mandatory Extra-Coarse Grind: Fine particles turn into muddy sludge during prolonged steeping, clogging filters and imparting harsh bitterness. Grind to coarse sea salt consistency (~1200 μm+).
  • Steep at 4°C Refrigeration: While room-temperature steeping is faster, cold refrigerator immersion prevents microbial proliferation and volatile lipid oxidation, yielding crisp clarity.
  • Dual-Stage Filtration Technique: Strain first through a stainless-steel mesh to catch large grinds, then pass through a rinsed paper V60 filter to remove all micro-fines for pristine clarity.

3. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q. Does cold brew contain less caffeine than hot coffee?

A. That is a widespread myth! While cold water dissolves caffeine slower than hot water, the extended 16-hour contact time results in high caffeine extraction. Uncut cold brew concentrate frequently contains significantly more caffeine per ounce than standard drip coffee.

Q. How long can cold brew concentrate be stored in the fridge?

A. Filtered cleanly into a sanitized airtight glass bottle, cold brew concentrate remains fresh for up to 14 days. Peak flavor typically emerges between Day 3 and 5.

Q. What happens if I steep cold brew for over 24 hours?

A. Exceeding 20 hours draws woody cellulose compounds and stale tannins out of the bean matrix, resulting in a dull, cardboard-like aftertaste. Always separate coffee grounds around the 16~18 hour mark.
